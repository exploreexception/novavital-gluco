Understanding the product:

NovaVital Gluco is a product name for glucose monitoring and is not a diagnosis. The traditional blood glucose metre is an electronic healthcare device used to check the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually takes disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to take a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre provides a number that can show the user what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not establish all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

Understanding How It Works

Most home-use blood glucose metres work with a test strip with a chemically treated area that responds with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is deposited on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre receives that signal and gives an answer, usually in the widely used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is not limited to the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can impact results. Therefore, carefully washing and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are essential steps of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems need specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be carried over to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be reviewed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Key Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it provides a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be conducted in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can give useful information about how glucose fluctuates during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Regular monitoring also may be more practical with a portable system. Modern metres retain past results and some systems feature features for the organization or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is dependent on the model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation behind them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by maintaining a record of readings over time. This is not a replacement for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be inaccurate if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can assist in detecting trends that might be hard to notice otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can cause needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be consistent to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to deliver a reading.

Important Limitations & Risks

Blood glucose metres are valuable monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be not identical from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another common source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all contribute to inaccurate readings.

The management of test strips is especially critical. Strips typically have storage specifications and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may pass their usable date after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should use the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be affected if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a practical risk with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is given it could cause someone to make an incorrect treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should continue with your instructions and ask a clinician for advice if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
