What the product is:

NovaVital Gluco is a product label for glucose monitoring and is not a diagnosis. The traditional blood glucose metre is an electronic glucose-testing instrument used to determine the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually uses disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to get a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre displays a number that can give someone an indication of what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not diagnose all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

How Does It Function

Most mobile blood glucose metres require a test strip with a chemically treated area that combines with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is deposited on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system generates a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre reads that signal and gives an answer, usually in the usual measurement units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is dependent on more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can change results. Therefore, proper hand washing and drying before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are critical components of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems require specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be extended to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be checked for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Potential Advantages

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it delivers a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be done in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide information about how glucose moves up and down during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Frequent monitoring also may be simpler to perform with a portable system. Modern metres keep past results and some systems feature features for the handling or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is variable between models and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation used with them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by giving a record of readings over time. This is not a alternative for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be inaccurate if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can reveal trends that might be challenging to detect otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can result in needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be appropriate to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to generate a reading.

Important Limitations & Risks

Blood glucose metres are practical monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be variable from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another significant source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all lead to inaccurate readings.

The handling of test strips is especially essential. Strips typically have storage requirements and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may reach their expiration after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should check the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be unreliable if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a practical risk with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is displayed it could cause someone to make an incorrect treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should follow your instructions and consult a health care professional if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
