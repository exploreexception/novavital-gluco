What the product is:

NovaVital Gluco is a product name for glucose monitoring and is not a medical treatment. The traditional blood glucose metre is an electronic glucose-testing instrument used to determine the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually works with disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to collect a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre produces a number that can help a person understand what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not determine all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

How Does It Work

Most handheld blood glucose metres work with a test strip with a chemically treated area that engages with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is placed on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system generates a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre reads that signal and produces an answer, usually in the commonly used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is not limited to the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can change results. Therefore, carefully washing and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are important elements of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems need specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be propagated to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be checked for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Potential Advantages

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it offers a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be done in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide information about how glucose changes during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Frequent monitoring also may be easier to manage with a portable system. Modern metres save past results and some systems add features for the handling or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is model dependent and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation supporting them.

Home monitoring can assist you in communicating with your health care professional by offering a record of readings over time. This is not a equivalent for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be deceptive if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can assist in detecting trends that might be difficult to identify otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can create needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be appropriate to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to give a reading.

Risks & Limitations of

Blood glucose metres are practical monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be distinct from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another common source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all contribute to inaccurate readings.

The maintenance of test strips is especially essential. Strips typically have storage specifications and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may reach their expiration after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should use the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be altered if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a minor safety concern with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is produced it could cause someone to make an unsuitable treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should adhere to your instructions and seek professional advice if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
