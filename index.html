Understanding the product:

NovaVital Gluco is a product label for glucose monitoring and is not a therapeutic intervention. The traditional blood glucose metre is an medical measuring device used to determine the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually takes disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to draw a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre returns a number that can help a person understand what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not identify all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

Understanding How It Works

Most handheld blood glucose metres use a test strip with a chemically treated area that reacts with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is placed on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system records a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre detects that signal and produces an answer, usually in the widely used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is dependent on more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can affect results. Therefore, washing and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are important elements of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems use specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be transferred to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be followed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Possible Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it offers a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be carried out in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide a clearer picture about how glucose changes during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Consistent monitoring also may be easier to manage with a portable system. Modern metres store past results and some systems include features for the management or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is variable between models and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation supporting them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by maintaining a record of readings over time. This is not a alternative for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be misleading if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can highlight trends that might be difficult to pick up otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can lead to needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be consistent to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to generate a reading.

Limitations & Potential Risks of

Blood glucose metres are useful monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be not identical from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another important source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all cause inaccurate readings.

The maintenance of test strips is especially critical. Strips typically have storage requirements and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may no longer be valid after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should check the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be affected if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a possible physical concern with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is produced it could cause someone to make an unwise treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should use your instructions and consult a health care professional if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
