What this means:

The metre usually uses disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to draw a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre gives a number that can help someone see what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. Understanding How It Works

Most compact blood glucose metres need a test strip with a chemically treated area that engages with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is deposited on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre gets that signal and gives an answer, usually in the widely used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is influenced by more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can affect results. Therefore, carefully washing and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are essential steps of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems need specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be carried over to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be reviewed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Potential Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it offers a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be conducted in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide a clearer picture about how glucose varies during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Frequent monitoring also may be easier to manage with a portable system. Modern metres store past results and some systems provide features for the arrangement or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is dependent on the model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation behind them.

Home monitoring can aid communication with your health care professional by supplying a record of readings over time. This is not a replacement for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be misleading if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can assist in detecting trends that might be easy to miss otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can lead to needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be suited to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to generate a reading.

Risks & Limitations of

Blood glucose metres are useful monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be different from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another common source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all contribute to inaccurate readings.

The storage of test strips is especially critical. Strips typically have recommended storage conditions and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. You should consult the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be altered if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a possible physical concern with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is produced it could cause someone to make an unsuitable treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should continue with your instructions and seek professional advice if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
