What the product is:

NovaVital Gluco is a product name for glucose monitoring and is not a treatment. The traditional blood glucose metre is an electronic medical device used to check the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually accepts disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to get a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre gives a number that can show the user what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not determine all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

Understanding How It Works

Most mobile blood glucose metres need a test strip with a chemically treated area that combines with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is applied on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre gets that signal and returns an answer, usually in the widely used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is influenced by more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can influence results. Therefore, cleaning and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are key factors of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems use specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be applied to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be checked for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Possible Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it gives a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be carried out in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide information about how glucose fluctuates during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Consistent monitoring also may be more practical with a portable system. Modern metres keep past results and some systems feature features for the handling or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is model dependent and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation behind them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by supplying a record of readings over time. This is not a substitute for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be misleading if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can help detect trends that might be difficult to identify otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can lead to needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be suited to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to deliver a reading.

Important Limitations & Risks

Blood glucose metres are useful monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be variable from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another big source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all cause inaccurate readings.

The handling of test strips is especially necessary. Strips typically have storage requirements and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may pass their usable date after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should use the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be unreliable if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a potential physical issue with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is reported it could cause someone to make an inappropriate treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should use your instructions and consult a health care professional if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
