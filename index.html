What the product is:

NovaVital Gluco is a brand name for glucose monitoring and is not a medical diagnosis. The traditional blood glucose metre is an medical measuring device used to check the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually takes disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to obtain a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre displays a number that can allow someone to see what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not confirm all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

How Does It Work

Most portable blood glucose metres need a test strip with a chemically treated area that engages with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is added on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system records a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre gets that signal and gives an answer, usually in the commonly used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is not determined solely by the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can affect results. Therefore, washing then drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are key factors of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems require specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be propagated to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be followed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Key Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it gives a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be conducted in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can offer insight about how glucose moves up and down during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Routine monitoring also may be simpler to perform with a portable system. Modern metres record past results and some systems feature features for the arrangement or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is specific to each model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation applied to them.

Home monitoring can aid communication with your health care professional by offering a record of readings over time. This is not a alternative for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be inaccurate if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can help detect trends that might be difficult to pick up otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can result in needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be appropriate to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to provide a reading.

Important Limitations & Risks

Blood glucose metres are valuable monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be different from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another significant source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all contribute to inaccurate readings.

The handling of test strips is especially significant. Strips typically have recommended storage conditions and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may expire after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should check the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be altered if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a minor safety concern with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is obtained it could cause someone to make an improper treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should observe your instructions and obtain professional guidance if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
