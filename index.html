How Does It Work

Most portable blood glucose metres use a test strip with a chemically treated area that reacts with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is placed on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre detects that signal and provides an answer, usually in the most common units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is dependent on more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can change results. Therefore, proper hand washing and drying before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are key factors of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems depend on specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be propagated to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be reviewed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Potential Advantages

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it provides a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be completed in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide information about how glucose moves up and down during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Regular monitoring also may be more convenient with a portable system. Modern metres retain past results and some systems provide features for the arrangement or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is determined by the model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation used with them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by offering a record of readings over time. This is not a stand-in for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be misinterpreted if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can highlight trends that might be easy to miss otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can result in needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be suited to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to deliver a reading.

Potential Drawbacks & Risks of

Blood glucose metres are practical monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be somewhat different from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another major source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all result in inaccurate readings.

The maintenance of test strips is especially necessary. Strips typically have storage specifications and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may reach their expiration after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should use the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be altered if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a physical risk with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is given it could cause someone to make an inappropriate treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should follow your instructions and seek professional advice if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
