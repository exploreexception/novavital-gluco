How Does It Operate

Most home-use blood glucose metres work with a test strip with a chemically treated area that combines with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is placed on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre reads that signal and gives an answer, usually in the commonly used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is dependent on more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can influence results. Therefore, cleaning and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are important considerations of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems need specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be extended to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be reviewed for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Key Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it offers a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be done in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can provide a clearer picture about how glucose rises and falls during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Consistent monitoring also may be more convenient with a portable system. Modern metres save past results and some systems add features for the management or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is determined by the model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation associated with them.

Home monitoring can assist you in communicating with your health care professional by giving a record of readings over time. This is not a alternative for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be inaccurate if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can assist in detecting trends that might be hard to notice otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can lead to needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be fitting to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to give a reading.

Potential Drawbacks & Risks of

Blood glucose metres are practical monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be not identical from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another important source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all result in inaccurate readings.

The care of test strips is especially essential. Strips typically have defined storage instructions and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may become expired after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should refer to the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be invalid if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a physical risk with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is displayed it could cause someone to make an inappropriate treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should follow your instructions and get medical guidance if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
