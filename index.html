What it is:

NovaVital Gluco is a commercial name for glucose monitoring and is not a medical treatment. The traditional blood glucose metre is an electronic testing device used to determine the concentration of glucose in a blood sample. The metre usually takes disposable test strips and, depending on the system, a lancing device and lancets to collect a tiny capillary blood sample from a fingertip.

The blood glucose metre displays a number that can help someone see what his or her blood glucose is at a certain time. People with diabetes or other needs related to glucose management typically have home metres, but the appropriate testing schedule depends on the individual medical circumstances. A metre alone does not identify all types of diabetes or the cause of an abnormal glucose result.

The name could also be mixed up with other Nova Biomedical products such as the StatStrip Glucose metre. How Does It Work

Most home-use blood glucose metres work with a test strip with a chemically treated area that interacts with glucose in a blood sample. A small amount of blood is added on the strip and an electrochemical or similar sensing system produces a signal related to the glucose concentration. The metre receives that signal and returns an answer, usually in the commonly used units in the United States.

The accuracy of a reading is more than the electronics inside the metre. Test strips, blood-sampling technique, storage conditions, expiration status, environmental conditions and user technique can influence results. Therefore, washing and drying the hands before testing, using an adequate sample and following the manufacturer’s instructions are essential steps of reliable home monitoring.

Some professional Nova glucose monitoring systems depend on specific specimen and quality control procedures. By default those attributes should not be transferred to a consumer device called NovaVital Gluco. The exact manual for the model in use should be examined for specimen type, operating temperature, strip requirements, calibration procedures and error messages.

Possible Benefits

The primary potential benefit of a blood glucose metre is that it provides a current glucose measurement without requiring every test to be carried out in a laboratory. If you have been instructed to check your glucose at home, regular readings can help reveal patterns about how glucose changes during the day and how readings are related to meals, activity, medication, illness or other situations.

Frequent monitoring also may be simpler to perform with a portable system. Modern metres keep past results and some systems offer features for the organization or transfer of readings. However, the value of these features is dependent on the model and stored numbers are only as useful as the testing technique and interpretation supporting them.

Home monitoring can make it easier to communicate with your health care professional by offering a record of readings over time. This is not a stand-in for clinical assessment, laboratory testing or professional advice. A single metre result may also be misinterpreted if the sample is contaminated or the strip is damaged or the result is affected by a known interference.

Another potential benefit is that glucose testing can reveal trends that might be easy to miss otherwise. But at the same time, too much testing, or taking individual readings out of context, can lead to needless worry. The frequency and timing of testing should be consistent to a person’s treatment plan, not the ability of the metre to generate a reading.

Limitations & Risks of

Blood glucose metres are beneficial monitoring devices, but are not laboratory analysers. The results could be somewhat different from laboratory measurements within the instrument accuracy limits. Another big source of variation is user technique. Insufficient blood, wet or dirty fingers, improperly stored strips, expired supplies or incorrect operating procedures can all lead to inaccurate readings.

The management of test strips is especially important. Strips typically have recommended storage conditions and expiration dates, and some products indicate additional restrictions once a container is opened. Some Nova Stat Strip test strips may reach their expiration after opening, but it is not possible to answer this question for all of the Nova-branded strips in the same way. You should follow the applicable strip package and manual. The result may be invalid if the strips are used outside the stated conditions.

There is also a minor safety concern with repeated finger stick testing such as soreness, minor bleeding, bruising or skin irritation. More importantly, if a wrong glucose result is obtained it could cause someone to make an unwise treatment decision. If you are on insulin or glucose lowering medication you should follow your instructions and obtain professional guidance if your readings are unexpectedly high or low, or if they are accompanied by concerning symptoms.
